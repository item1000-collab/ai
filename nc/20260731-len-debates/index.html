📖 Structured lesson · thematic blocks

🎯 Target vocabulary & core ideas

  • Hubris – overstepping boundaries, cosmic arrogance
  • Nemesis – divine retribution, restoring balance
  • Metron (μέτρον) – limit, measure, the golden mean
  • Moirai / Fates – Clotho, Lachesis, Atropos
  • Cosmos – ordered universe, not random
  • Pious / piety – acting with moral duty, accepting limits
  • Border‑crossing – violating the line between mortal and divine

🧭 Thematic blocks (from discussion)

  • Cosmic ecosystem – gods, humans, nature in balance
  • Human drive – curiosity, excess, and the impulse to exceed
  • Golden mean – Aristotle, balance, and the virtuous path
  • Destiny vs. free will – the Fates, personal choice, and modern views
  • Restoration – nemesis as a cosmic thermostat

✨ Advanced vocabulary & idioms

reign supreme to hold the highest power without rivals
vie (vied) to compete intensely for dominance
cocky / overconfident arrogantly self‑assured, bragging
inflated ego exaggerated sense of self‑importance
border‑crossing violating established limits or roles
righteous indignation moral anger at injustice or excess
inexorable unstoppable, unrelenting (as Atropos)
cosmic thermostat metaphor for nemesis as a self‑regulating balance

📌 Summary · part 1
The session deconstructs the Greek concepts of hubris and nemesis beyond modern simplifications. Hubris is not mere arrogance but a violent transgression of cosmic measure (metron) — a crime against the divine order, often punished by the Fates. The discussion highlights how the Greeks saw the universe as a delicate ecosystem where every being has a fixed place, and exceeding that place invites nemesis: the righteous restoration of balance.

📌 Summary · part 2
Through mythological case studies and philosophical debate, the group explores destiny vs. free will, the golden mean, and the human impulse to exceed boundaries. Vocabulary work focuses on expressions like reign supreme, vie, and cosmic thermostat. The session bridges ancient ethics with modern language, emphasising that humility, self‑knowledge, and acceptance of limits are not weakness but wisdom — a shield against the fall that follows hubris.
